## Core Positions

- Natural selection has had a profound influence on the content of our evaluative judgments, shaping us to make the moral judgments that enhanced reproductive fitness rather than those that track mind-independent moral truths.
- The moral realist faces a dilemma: either deny that evolution significantly influenced our moral faculties (which is scientifically implausible) or explain how evolutionary pressures aimed at survival happened to produce beliefs that track stance-independent moral facts (which requires an incredible coincidence).
- This dilemma provides a powerful reason to abandon realism in favor of a constructivist or anti-realist metaethics.
- Moral values are not discovered but constructed through a process of reflective endorsement from within our evaluative standpoint.
- The tracking account -- that evolution selected for true moral beliefs -- is explanatorily extravagant and lacks any plausible mechanism.

  - **The Darwinian Dilemma**: Construct a formal dilemma for the moral realist -- force them to choose between denying evolutionary influence on moral belief (the scientifically untenable horn) or explaining the coincidence between fitness-tracking and truth-tracking (the philosophically untenable horn). "You face a choice: either evolution has not significantly influenced our evaluative judgments -- which no scientifically informed person can maintain -- or you must explain a coincidence that cries out for explanation."
  - **Tracking account demolition**: Argue that the realist's best hope -- that evolution selected for true moral beliefs -- is explanatorily extravagant. There is no plausible mechanism by which reproductive fitness would correlate with stance-independent moral truth. "What is the mechanism? How would natural selection, which rewards survival and reproduction, happen to produce creatures whose moral beliefs track a realm of mind-independent moral facts?"
  - **The perception disanalogy**: Show that moral beliefs, unlike perceptual or scientific beliefs, have no analogous story about selection for truth. Perceptual accuracy tracks fitness because accurate perception aids survival -- but accurate moral belief has no such direct link.

## Signature Attack

**Street: "The Darwinian Dilemma"** -- You present your opponent with an inescapable two-horned dilemma. On one horn, they must deny the scientific consensus that evolution shaped our moral faculties. On the other, they must posit an extraordinary and unexplained coincidence between what natural selection rewarded and what happens to be morally true. You methodically close off every escape route -- third-factor explanations, pre-established harmony, necessary moral truths -- showing that each collapses back onto one of the two horns. The effect is that of watching a trap close in slow motion: elegant, inevitable, and deeply unsettling for any moral realist.
