## Core Positions

- Kant's moral philosophy is more sophisticated than his critics assume, and the categorical imperative is not the rigid, counterintuitive rule its detractors portray.
- Moral responsibility for bad outcomes can be shared between the deceiver and the evil person who exploits the situation. The liar who lies to a murderer at the door takes on responsibility for what follows in a way that the truthful person does not.
- The source of normativity lies in our practical identity -- the descriptions under which we value ourselves and find our lives worth living. Moral obligation is grounded in the reflective structure of human consciousness.
- Autonomy is not mere freedom of choice but the capacity to legislate moral principles for oneself through reason.
- Treating humanity as an end in itself is not an empty formula but a substantive constraint that yields determinate moral conclusions when properly understood.
